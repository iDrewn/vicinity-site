Privacy

Privacy policy

VIcinity · Last updated 19 November 2026 · Svenska

VIcinity has no ads, no third-party analytics and no tracking, and it never asks for your name, e-mail address, phone number or location. It does have accounts and a server, because the chart, the cheats, the progress boards and the tips are shared between players. This page says exactly what the server stores, why, and how you can remove it.

Who is responsible

Anders Johansson, a private person in Sweden, makes VIcinity and runs its server. I am the controller of the personal data described here. Contact: see Questions below.

Accounts

The first time you open VIcinity, the app creates a guest account with a random handle such as “Player4821”. You can change the handle in Settings. A guest account has no sign-in method: it is tied to a random secret kept in your phone’s Keychain. You can later add either of these, or both:

  • Sign in with Apple. VIcinity asks Apple for no name and no e-mail address. The server stores only the opaque identifier Apple gives the app for you.
  • A passkey. The server stores the public part of the passkey. The private part stays on your devices and in your iCloud Keychain, under Apple’s terms, and I cannot see it.

What the server stores

DataWhy
Your handle, when the account was made and when you were last seen, your app version, and which sign-in methods you haveTo run your account, to show your name on what you share, and to make sure the app and server work together.
A sign-in session for each device: a hash of its secret, a label such as “apple” or “guest”, and when it was last usedTo keep you signed in. Signing out or deleting the account removes it.
What you share: pins (title, description, category, rarity, position and an optional screenshot), cheats, your confirmations and disputes, thanks you give, and reports you fileThis is the shared chart and the cheat list. Your handle is shown with it.
Your progress: the milestones you tick and when (the server’s clock), how many items you have found in each collection, and an optional screenshot if you attach one as proofTo track your progress and to rank players on the leaderboards. You can hide yourself from the leaderboards in Settings.
Your subscription status: the product, its expiry and Apple’s transaction numberTo unlock VIcinity Pro on every device and to enforce it on the server.
Moderation records: whether an account is muted or banned and why, a staff note, and a log of what staff didTo keep the community safe and to be able to show what was done and by whom.

What the server does not collect: your name, e-mail address, phone number, contacts, advertising identifier, device identifiers beyond your handle and session, or your location. VIcinity does not use your GPS. Pin positions are places on the chart, not places in the real world.

What other players see

Your handle, the pins and cheats you share, the screenshots you attach to pins, the thanks your tips receive, your tipster rank, and, unless you hide yourself, your position and percentage on the leaderboards. Progress screenshots you attach as proof are seen only by me and by moderators.

On your device

VIcinity keeps a copy of the chart and cheats so it opens quickly and works offline, plus things that belong to this phone only: which pins you marked as found, your route, the players you blocked and your settings. Your session secret is kept in the Keychain and does not leave the device in readable form. Deleting the app removes the local copy.

Where the server runs

The server runs on a computer in my home in Sweden. It is reached through Cloudflare’s tunnel service, which therefore handles your connection and can see its technical data, such as the IP address, on the way. Cloudflare acts as my processor under its own terms. The server does not store your IP address: it uses it only in memory, to limit how fast one network can create accounts or try to sign in. The database is backed up on the same computer, and old backups are deleted after about two weeks.

Apple

Sign in with Apple, passkeys, iCloud Keychain, the App Store and your subscription are Apple’s services under Apple’s own privacy policy. VIcinity Pro is bought through the App Store. Apple handles the payment and I never receive your payment details. The app sends the server a signed transaction from Apple so the server can check that your subscription is real. If you install VIcinity through TestFlight or the App Store, Apple may also collect usage and crash information under Apple’s policy. I see only what Apple chooses to share, such as tester feedback.

The GTABase map

On the chart, a button shows a separate map from GTABase (gtabase.com) inside the app. That page is loaded straight from GTABase and not through my server. GTABase can see your IP address and uses its own cookies, under its own privacy policy. VIcinity does not read or store anything from that page. You choose whether to open it.

Reports, blocking and moderation

You can report any pin, cheat or player. A report stores which item you reported, your handle and your reason, and it is seen only by me and by moderators. Content that doesn’t belong may be hidden or removed, and accounts that break the rules may be muted or banned. Blocking a player hides their content on your phone only.

How long I keep it

Your account and progress stay until you delete your account. Guest accounts that are never used again may be removed after a long time without activity. Backups are deleted after about two weeks.

Deleting your account

In VIcinity, open Progress, then Settings, then Delete account. This removes your sign-in methods, sessions, progress, collection counts, proof screenshots and any access granted outside the App Store. Pins and cheats you shared stay on the chart without your name, because other players have confirmed and built on them. If you want a particular pin or screenshot removed too, ask me (see Questions) and I will remove it. If you used Sign in with Apple, you can also stop sharing it with the app in Settings → your name → Sign-In & Security → Sign in with Apple.

Your rights

You can ask me what the server has stored about you, ask me to correct or delete it, and object to how I use it. In practice most of this you can do yourself in the app: change your handle, hide yourself from leaderboards, delete your account. Because guest accounts have no name or e-mail address, I may ask you to show me your handle from inside the app so I can find your record. The legal basis for the processing is that it is needed to provide the app to you, and my legitimate interest in running a safe, fair community and preventing abuse. You can complain to the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY, imy.se).

Children

VIcinity is not made for children and I do not knowingly collect data from children under 13. Player-made content is moderated, but it is not curated for younger players.

Not affiliated

VIcinity is an independent fan app. It is not made, endorsed or sponsored by any game publisher or developer, and cheat codes, milestones and map data in it are shared by players or gathered from public guides and may be wrong.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with an updated date.

Questions

Open an issue on the project’s GitHub page and I will answer there. Please do not put personal details in a public issue: say that you have a privacy request, and I will tell you how to send it to me privately.
